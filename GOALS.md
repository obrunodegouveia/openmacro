# What OpenMacro is for

> **The goal:** understand money deeply enough that two things become true of
> you. You can run your own financial life on the most efficient terms
> available to you, because you understand what every one of those terms
> actually is. And you could sit in the chair of the president of a central
> bank — any central bank, in any country — and do the job on the technical
> merits.

Those are not two goals. They are the same knowledge, checked at two
different scales.

The person who can read a central bank's balance sheet line by line is the
same person who reads their own mortgage contract and knows precisely which
risk they have just been sold. The mechanism that sets a policy rate is the
mechanism that reprices their loan. The reason a bank fails in a weekend is
the reason a deposit above the insured limit is not the same asset as one
below it. Learn it once, properly, and it pays at both ends.

---

## The first proof: your own money

Most financial advice is a list of instructions to follow. This course is not
that, and refuses to be, because instructions expire and understanding does
not.

What it gives you instead is the ability to answer questions about your own
money from first principles:

- **Where is my money, and whose promise is it?** The balance in your banking
  app is not money in a vault. It is a liability of a commercial bank — its
  promise to you — and knowing what stands behind that promise, and what
  happens when it fails, changes what you do with it.
- **What is this contract really charging me?** A rate is a price for time and
  risk. Once you can decompose one, a mortgage, a credit card, a car finance
  deal and a government bond stop being different products and become the same
  product at different prices.
- **What is inflation doing to me specifically?** Not the headline number — the
  one your own basket produces, which is a different number, and the reason the
  published figure can fall while nothing you buy gets cheaper.
- **When should I not act?** The most expensive financial decisions are usually
  made in the weeks when acting feels most urgent. Understanding why an asset
  price moved is what separates a reason from a panic.
- **What is being sold to me, and by whom?** Almost every financial product is
  a transfer of risk. Knowing which risk, in which direction, and who is paid
  for carrying it, is most of the defence.

The claim is not that a course makes anyone rich. It is that the largest
avoidable losses in an ordinary financial life come from not understanding the
instrument in front of you — and that this is fixable, by learning the
mechanisms rather than memorising the advice.

**This is education, not financial advice.** Nothing here is a recommendation
to buy, sell or hold anything, and no part of it is tailored to your
circumstances. What it offers is the understanding you would want *before*
taking anyone's advice, including ours.

## The second proof: the institution

The technical bar is deliberately absolute: **a proficient student could run
any central bank in the world.**

Not "could follow the debate". Not "could hold an informed opinion". Could do
the job. That means, concretely, being able to:

- Read the Fed's and the ECB's balance sheets line by line, and say what moved
  and why
- Set a policy rate, and defend the choice against the rule, the forecast and
  the data you actually had at the time rather than the revised data
- Know which lever moves which rate, through which channel, with which lag,
  and why the same decision lands nine times harder in one country than
  another
- Run the payment system, judge collateral, and price a haircut
- Tell an illiquid institution from an insolvent one at four in the morning,
  and know what each one requires of you
- Defend a currency, and know what the defence costs before it starts and when
  it cannot be won
- Hold the line with a finance ministry on the other end of the telephone

The course is explicit about what it does **not** cover: the political and
communication craft of the office. Those matter enormously and they are a
different discipline. What is here is the technical core — the part where
being wrong is a measurable failure rather than a matter of judgement about
which reasonable people differ.

## Why the bar is set there

Because a bar set anywhere lower produces vocabulary instead of understanding.

It is easy to write a course that leaves someone able to use the words
*quantitative easing* in a sentence. It is much harder to leave them able to
post the entries, say whose balance sheet expanded, and explain why the same
operation does something different in an economy where households hold
fixed-rate mortgages. The second person understands money. The first has
learned a phrase.

Setting the bar at "could run the institution" forces every lesson to be the
second kind. If a lesson would not survive contact with somebody who does this
for a living, it is not finished. A lesson that teaches a vocabulary word
instead of a mechanism is not finished either.

## How the course is built to get you there

**Three levels, in order.** Beginner establishes what money is and where it
comes from. Intermediate covers the instruments and the markets they trade in.
Advanced is the operating manual for the institution: policy implementation,
financial stability, crisis machinery, the target variable and the framework
around it.

**Mechanisms, not definitions.** Every lesson aims at something you can *do* —
post the double entry, drive the simulation until the trade-off is visible,
put the causal chain in order. Roughly half the course is this kind of applied
work rather than recall, and the exam holds that ratio deliberately.

**Numbers you can check.** The simulations compute real arithmetic from real
parameters. When a lesson says a rate rise takes nine times as much household
income in one country as another, that is a calculation you can run yourself
by moving the sliders, not a claim you are asked to accept.

**It is a game, and that is not a compromise.** Hearts, XP, streaks, a wrong
answer re-queued so you cannot leave a lesson until you can do every step. The
loop exists because the fastest way to genuine competence is to keep someone
coming back, and because a twelve-year-old should want the next lesson.

## How proficiency is demonstrated

Finishing the lessons is not the same as being able to do the job, so the
course separates the two.

The exam is drawn from the whole course rather than the module you just
revised, sat once with no second attempt per question, and assembled
deterministically from a seed — so the exact paper can be rebuilt and checked
by someone who was not present.

It is **gated by level**, which is the part that matters. An overall
percentage hides a candidate who aced the easy material and failed everything
advanced. Every level carries its own threshold and all of them must be met,
so passing means passing the whole thing.

## Who this is for

**Anyone who uses money**, which is the honest answer, and specifically:

- **Teenagers and young adults** who were never taught where money comes from,
  and are about to make the largest borrowing decisions of their lives
- **Parents, guardians and teachers**, who are a first-class audience rather
  than an afterthought — the course is built to be run at a kitchen table or
  in a classroom
- **Anyone who works near money** — in a bank, a treasury, a fintech, a
  newsroom — and has been operating on the vocabulary rather than the
  mechanism
- **Anyone who intends to do this for a living.** The bar is set where it is
  precisely so that this is not an unreasonable use of the course.

## The measure of success

Not downloads.

The number of people who can explain, over dinner, where the money in their
account came from and whose liability it is — and, for some of them, the day
one sits an exam built to the standard above and passes it.
